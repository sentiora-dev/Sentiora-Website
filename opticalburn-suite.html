01 / Burning

Six ways onto a disc, four of them free.

Data. An ISO, bootable ones included. A real Red Book audio CD that plays in a fifteen-year-old car stereo. A USB stick written sector by sector. Then two for the licence holders: MultiDisc, and raw SCSI writing for when you want your hands on the drive.

02 / The everyday job

You can see whether it fits before you find out.

The gauge fills as you drag files in. Save the list and load it again next month, instead of rebuilding it from memory. The settings underneath are the ones that matter on the day: finalise or leave it open, verify afterwards, write the checksum list onto the disc alongside the files, or stage everything to the hard drive first so a slow source cannot starve the burn.

03 / Media

700 MB to 100 GB.

Pick what you are burning to and the gauge recalculates against it. The speeds on offer are the ones your drive says it can do — read out of the drive, not copied from a list somebody wrote once.

04 / A real job, running

76 GB, four discs, no arithmetic on your part.

It measures the disc that is actually in the drive — 22.6 GB, not the 25 on the box — works out that four are needed, and tells you the last one will be about a third full. Then it asks for the next disc when it wants it. If you have two drives it will use both and never ask at all. Underneath: how fast it is really going, how full the drive's buffer is, and how much longer.

05 / Rescue

A scratched disc does not die all at once.

Sectors die. The recovery pass reads through them one at a time, notes the ones that refuse and keeps going instead of stalling at the first bad patch, then comes back to the skipped ones up to eight times. Whatever still will not read is written down, with the exact position of every lost sector. You are never told "finished" over a file with a hole in it.

06 / ISO and audio

Images, with the tray shut.

Build an ISO now and burn it whenever. Turn a BIN, an IMG or an old Nero NRG into a plain ISO any program will open. Pull files out of an image without burning it or mounting it. And take an audio CD apart into MP3, FLAC or WAV — name the tracks yourself, or leave them numbered.

07 / Light or dark

Whichever you can stand at two in the morning.

One click in the title bar. And every screen, every message, every warning exists in twelve languages — Romanian, English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic, Chinese, Hindi and Bengali.

/ All twenty functions

Everything that gets you to install it is free.

Burning goes through Windows' own IMAPI engine, the same one the system uses, so nothing installs a driver of its own or wedges itself between Windows and your drive. Raw writing and disc diagnosis are the exceptions — those talk to the drive directly, over SCSI, because there is no other way to do them.

02 / RESCUE

The half you buy a licence for

  • Erase disc — empties a rewritable one. Free, because free burning would be no use without it Free
  • Copy disc — onto another disc, or into an image on the hard drive
  • Recover data — sector by sector, skipping the dead ones so it never stalls, then returning to them up to eight times
  • CD recovery (C2) — a second pass over only what is left. Sub-block reads, the drive's own C2 error information, up to sixteen attempts, taking whichever answer comes back most often. The last thing to try
  • Verify disc — the written disc against the image it came from, byte by byte

04 / TOOLS

For the shelf you open in ten years

  • Reed-Solomon protection — the file cut into 16 slices, with up to 32 spares kept beside it. If it rots later, the spares rebuild it. The insurance you wish you had taken out
  • And it checks the rebuilt file against fingerprints taken while it was still whole. If they disagree it says it failed and deletes the result, rather than handing you something that only looks intact
  • Encrypt / decrypt — AES-256-GCM, key derived by PBKDF2-SHA256 over 600,000 rounds, every chunk numbered and authenticated so nobody can quietly remove or reorder one. No back door: forget the password and it is gone, to us as well
  • File checksum — CRC32, MD5 or SHA-256
  • Disc diagnosis — what is really in the drive: blank or written, how many sessions, where writing can continue, how much is left
  • Operations journal — what you did, when, how long it took, how it ended Free

/ Release

Verified, not assumed.

Verification does not mean "written without an error". It means the disc is read back and compared against what should be on it, byte by byte — and if the check does not reach the end, for whatever reason, it says so plainly instead of quietly passing. The fingerprint list is saved on the disc beside your files as an ordinary SHA256SUMS.txt. In ten years you can check that disc on any computer, with certutil on Windows or sha256sum on Linux, without this program anywhere in sight.
