01 / Health · S.M.A.R.T.

A drive that is doing well.

Health, wear, spare blocks, media errors, unsafe shutdowns, temperature and hours powered on — taken from the drive itself, not estimated. Above them sits the line people actually want: how long it has left, and a trend showing whether the reading is holding or sliding. Reads only.

02 / A drive that is failing

Back up now, not later.

Over seven million reallocated sectors, thirty-two pending, and CRC errors on the cable. The same numbers other tools print and leave you to interpret; here they come with a verdict — replace the disk — and an honest estimate in days to weeks. SMART is a strong indication, not a prophecy, and the wording says so.

03 / Surface scan

Read every sector, write nothing.

Green where the drive answers normally, yellow where it is slow, red where it errors — and a failed block is narrowed to 4 KB so it can name the exact address. The full scan walks the whole disk and finds isolated bad sectors, in hours. The quick scan samples about every 25 MB and finishes in minutes, which finds slow regions and large damage but cannot find an isolated sector. Reads only.

04 / Check and repair

Verify without touching anything.

Verify-only runs while the volume stays in use and changes nothing at all, so it is safe whenever you like. Repair marks bad sectors so Windows stops using them — that modifies the disk, so it stays behind ARMED mode and locks the volume while it works.

05 / Benchmark

How fast the drive really is.

256 MB read sequentially in 4 MB chunks, then random access at 4K, 64K and 1 MB: how quickly it copies large files, and how quickly it starts programs. It is how you tell a tired SSD from a healthy one. It writes nothing whatsoever.

06 / Ask Sentiora AI

A conversation about this machine.

It opens already holding a picture of the hardware — every drive, its health and temperature, the graphics card, the memory — so "is my SSD dying?" is answered about your SSD, with your numbers. It remembers what you have told it, and asks a short question only when the answer genuinely depends on something it cannot know. It can offer you a tool, but you press the button, and the tools it may offer are read-only ones — a whitelist in the code, not a polite instruction.

07 / Your provider, your key

Off until you switch it on.

The advisor does nothing until you paste in a key of your own, at a provider of your own: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Mistral, Groq, DeepSeek, or a model running on your own machine. You pay them directly, at their prices — Sentiora resells no access and runs no AI service, and nothing passes through it. The key is encrypted on your PC, and each service keeps its own.

08 / System detection

Check its work yourself.

Processor, memory down to DDR4 or DDR5 and its speed, BIOS, every drive with the SMART method used to read it, and the graphics card. A tick for what it read, a question mark for what it could not — which is how you satisfy yourself that it has understood your machine before you trust anything else it says. Reads only.

09 / Driver Finder

Microsoft's sources, and nowhere else.

It finds the parts without a working driver and looks each one up by its own hardware ID, through the Microsoft Update Catalog and Windows Update. Never a third-party driver site. Finding is a read; installing what it finds modifies the system, so that part needs a licence.

10 / DLL Doctor

The reason the game will not start.

It recognises the "x.dll is missing" error, works out which official Microsoft package you are actually short of, and gives you the official address for it. It never downloads loose DLL files from the web, which is one of the commoner ways a machine gets infected. Diagnosis reads; installing the package writes.

11 / Automated test

It checks itself, function by function.

Ticks the disks you choose and runs the application's own functions against them, quietly in the background or with the windows shown. Bad sectors found on the way are reported as a disk warning rather than an application fault, and the advisor can read the finished report if you have one connected.

12 / ARMED mode

Simulation until you say otherwise.

Cloning, formatting, initialising, secure erase and the write-based tests do nothing at all while the application is in simulation — which is how it starts, every time. Armed, the bar turns orange and stays orange, each operation asks again by name, and formatting wants a confirmation code containing the disk number, in your language.

13 / Disks and tools

Everything in one row.

Health report, health history, system detection, Driver Finder and DLL Doctor — the ones that only look. Secure erase and format sit apart on their own line, because they are the ones that do not.

/ Capabilities

Days, or years. It tells you which.

The reading is taken from the drive itself over SMART, with several methods tried in turn — a direct command to the disk, the SMART built into Windows, reliability counters. If none of them work on your drive it says so, rather than inventing a number. The scale is calibrated against a real dying disk, not chosen because it looks tidy.
